# Market review: what the leading gym trackers do, and what Gym Tracker should take

Written 2026-10-14, against build `2026-10-08d`. It is based on the apps' own store
listings, feature pages and help docs, plus independent reviews (sources at the
end), not on hands-on testing. Feature claims are the vendors' own.

**Everything under "Proposals" is a suggestion, not agreed work.** Agreed work lives
in `docs/BACKLOG.md`. Confirm an item with Ross before building it, then move it there.

The review is organised around the three things Ross values most:
**designing effective workouts**, **tracking progression**, and **visualising
progress**.

## The apps, in one line each

| App | Known for | The idea worth taking |
|---|---|---|
| **Hevy** | The most popular free logger, with a social feed | Per-exercise graphs of best weight, volume and total reps; muscle-distribution chart over 30 days / 3 months / 1 year / all time; workout calendar |
| **Strong** | Fastest, simplest logging | Warm-up calculator that suggests warm-up weights; plate calculator; set tags (warm-up, failure, drop); supersets |
| **Alpha Progression** | A per-set progression coach | Double progression with set-by-set targets; recommendations can hold or drop when performance doesn't support a jump; uses the weights you actually have; mesocycles and deloads; you choose the performance metric it shows in the logger, history and records (1RM, 10RM or volume) |
| **Fitbod** | Workouts generated by an algorithm | Weekly set targets per muscle group (it cites roughly 10–20 working sets per muscle per week); colour-coded recovery map per muscle |
| **Boostcamp** | A big library of proven programmes | Multi-week programmes; volume heatmap; a weekly report listing the PRs hit |
| **Liftosaur** | Programmes written as text, with scripted progression | Weekly volume per muscle group shown *while you design the programme*; a different progression rule per exercise (linear or double); progression switched off for deload weeks |
| **RP Hypertrophy** | Autoregulated volume | Feedback on pump, soreness and workload adjusts the plan week by week |
| **JEFIT** | A huge exercise library with video | Estimated 1RM analytics. Its reviews also show the cost of needing a connection: users complain it won't start offline |

## What the market agrees on

1. **Logging speed decides whether an app gets used.** Strong's whole pitch is
   speed. The original brief in `docs/ROADMAP.md` set two taps per set as the bar.
2. **Insight beats storage.** Reviews praise progress charts that tell you whether
   you are improving, and criticise apps that only store data.
3. **Progression guidance is where the apps now compete.** Alpha Progression,
   Fitbod, RP and the newer Hevy Trainer all tell you what to lift next rather than
   just recording it.
4. **Volume per muscle group per week** is the standard unit for judging whether a
   plan is balanced: Fitbod targets it, Hevy and Boostcamp chart it, and Liftosaur
   shows it while you build the plan.

## Where Gym Tracker already stands

Most of the table stakes and several of the differentiators are already built:

- Fast logging: prefill from last time, ± steppers, two taps per set, untyped-reps
  check on finish
- Set types (warm-up, AMRAP, drop), warm-up rows, supersets, auto rest timer
- Plate calculator, per-exercise notes, form cues, swaps, home/gym kit
- **A double-progression coach on every card**, with a reason for every "stay",
  stall and back-off detection, kit-specific weight jumps, and off-day handling.
  This is the Alpha Progression model: a stable plan, progressed set by set
- Three kinds of PB (weight, reps at weight, rep-range records), estimated 1RM,
  up/flat/down trends
- Weekly sets per muscle group against the plan, Monday check-in, and "How you're
  doing" span comparisons
- Routines, three presets, a plan editor, history editing, bodyweight chart
- Offline service worker, automatic backup on finish, JSON/CSV export, no account

What's thinnest, compared with the leaders, is **visualisation**: one chart type
(top set plus an estimated 1RM line) with no time range, and muscle-group volume
for the current week only. **Plan design** is next: the editor shows sets per
session but not per muscle per week, and the plan is fixed at three days.

## Proposals

Priority: **P1** is the clearest gap for what Ross values, **P2** is worth doing,
**P3** is "later, if ever". An item marked *(backlog)* is already in
`docs/BACKLOG.md`. It is listed here because the review supports it.

### Designing effective workouts

- **P1. Weekly sets per muscle group in the plan editor.** As days and sets are
  edited, show the planned working sets per group per week across all days.
  `plannedByGroup()` in `61-week.js` already computes this. It is Liftosaur's
  best idea, and it turns the editor from a list into a design tool. A reference
  band (e.g. Fitbod's ~10–20) could be shown as guidance only. The in-session
  views keep judging against the plan's own numbers, as agreed on 2026-10-07.
- **P1. Finer muscle groups with secondary muscles** *(backlog D)*. This is what
  makes the item above honest: dumbbell bench should count towards triceps and
  front delts too.
- **P2. Day editor: add, rename and remove days** *(backlog)*. Every leader
  supports any split. A 4th day was already tried once (see `TRAINING-PLAN.md`).
- **P2. Swap to any library exercise, not only the listed alternates**
  *(backlog)*.
- **P3. Deload week.** One toggle that runs a week at about half the sets and
  turns off the coach's judgement for it (Liftosaur, Alpha Progression).
  Low value for a beginner who is still progressing every session.
- **P3. A progression rule per exercise** (linear for a barbell lift, double
  progression elsewhere, as in Liftosaur). Only if the double-progression coach
  turns out to be wrong for a specific lift.

### Tracking progression

- **P1. Per-set history editing, and per-set CSV** *(backlog, loose ends)*. All the
  leaders let you correct any past set. Today, editing changes only `top`/`reps`
  and the two can disagree with `sets`.
- **P2. Suggested warm-up weights.** Fill the warm-up rows with a ramp up to the
  working weight (e.g. ~50% and ~75%), snapped to the kit's jumps. Strong and Hevy
  both offer this. Today the rows start blank, which costs typing on every
  exercise.
- **P2. Session note** *(backlog E)*.
- **P2. Place-aware PBs** *(backlog, gym/home leftover)*. A home dumbbell
  shouldn't count as a gym PB.
- **P3. Reps in reserve on the last set** *(backlog E)*. Only if the effort
  rating proves too coarse, as already agreed.

### Visualising progress

- **P1. Time range on the exercise chart.** Add chips for 4 weeks, 3 months and
  all time, reusing `SPANS` from `63-compare.js`. The hand-rolled SVG is noted as
  "fine to ~20 points". At three sessions a week since mid-August, the main lifts
  are at that limit now. Hevy offers the same ranges.
- **P1. Metric switch on the exercise chart.** Choose top set (the default, as
  Ross picked it), estimated 1RM, session volume, or best reps. Hevy and Alpha
  Progression both let you choose. The data is already in each history entry.
- **P1. "Then vs now" line on the chart sheet** *(backlog C, still open)*.
- **P2. Muscle-group sets over time.** Weekly working sets per group for the last
  8–12 weeks, as small bars per group. This is Hevy's muscle distribution over
  time and Boostcamp's volume heatmap. Today only the current week is shown.
- **P2. Training calendar.** A month-by-month grid of sessions, marked gym/home,
  beyond the 7-day strip. Hevy pairs a workout calendar with a streak for
  consistency.
- **P2. Milestones and a monthly report** *(backlog C)*. Boostcamp's weekly PR
  report is the market version.
- **P3. Relative strength** *(backlog C)*: top set ÷ bodyweight for the main lifts.
- **P3. Muscle map.** Fitbod's and Hevy's body heatmaps. Costly to draw well in
  inline SVG, and the per-group bars carry the same information.

## Deliberately not taking

- **Social feeds and sharing** (Hevy). One user.
- **Algorithm-generated workouts** (Fitbod). They work against what Ross values:
  designing the plan himself and keeping it stable enough to progress on.
- **Accounts and cloud sync.** Local ownership and offline use are strengths
  here, and JEFIT's reviews show the cost of needing a connection. Copying
  backups off the phone (backlog F) solves the real risk without an account.
- **Nutrition and wearables.**
- **Progress photos** (Hevy). Images would crowd out history within
  localStorage's few-MB quota and bloat every backup.
- **Exercise video libraries** (JEFIT, Alpha Progression). The app is one offline
  file. Short form cues already cover the need.
